A schoolyard line · one book

Hjelp Amanda

Amanda and Bodil take turns claiming prizes from the end of a line. The only prize Amanda wants is a book of fishes, and it sits at the front — so it is taken last. She may choose who starts. There is a way for her to make the book hers, however long the line is.

  • Take 1 to 6 From the end of the line only. You must take at least one. You cannot touch the front until it is the end.
  • The book is first It is prize 1. Whoever takes the last stretch of the line, six prizes or fewer, takes the book with them.
  • Amanda names the opener She waits until the prizes are laid out, sees how many there are, and decides whether she or Bodil moves first.

    Why seven

    Number the prizes from the book. After a take, the prize left at the end has a number equal to how many prizes remain. Amanda always leaves a multiple of 7 at the end — prize 7, 14, 21, and so on. Those are the traps. The player who has to move from one of them must break it, and the other player can finish the group of seven.

    Fourteen prizes, counted from the book. The tide stones are the traps. Amanda does not open this line; she lets Bodil break 14, then she lands the end on 7, and takes the book on the visit after that.

    The argument

    1. 1 through 6 are wins. The player to move takes the whole line. The book is in that take.
    2. 7 is a loss. Every legal take leaves 1 through 6, and the opponent takes the book.
    3. The pattern continues. If every multiple of 7 so far is a loss, and every other count is a win, then the next multiple of 7 is a loss too: each take leaves a non-multiple, which the opponent can answer by restoring a multiple of 7. And each of the six counts after that multiple is a win, by taking exactly the extra and leaving the multiple.
    4. Amanda chooses the winning side. She moves first when the length is not a multiple of 7, and second when it is. On every turn of hers she takes remaining mod 7 prizes. That number is always between 1 and 6, and it always leaves a multiple of 7 for Bodil — until the line is short enough to clear.
    Bodil takes the book whenever her turn starts with six or fewer prizes left. The plan above does not need her to blunder: if she plays the rest of the line as carefully as she likes, the book still ends with Amanda.
