P-positions and N-positions

Label every position by what happens to the player about to move.

N-position

Next player wins

There is at least one move to a P-position. In our game: any r not divisible by 7.

P-position

Previous player wins

Every move leads to an N-position. In our game: 0, 7, 14, 21, …

Two rules generate everything

(1) Terminal positions (no moves) are P. (2) A position is N iff some move reaches a P-position; otherwise P.

Backward induction, drawn

Solve from the end of the game towards the start. For r = 10 (the assignment's example):

0 P nobody can move 1 N take 1 → 0 2 N take 2 → 0 · … 6 N take 6 → 0 7 P every move lands on 1…6, all N 8 N take 1 → 7 9 N take 2 → 7 10 N take 3 → 7 ← Amanda's opening in the example

Each line is justified using only the lines above it. That's why a short table of the first 14 positions is already a proof sketch for all n.

Grundy numbers (the mex machine)

For each position, look at the Grundy numbers of the positions you can reach, and take the mex: the smallest non-negative integer that is missing. Zero ⇔ P-position. Here, the value cycles 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6 and repeats.

Why bother? Grundy numbers let you add games: if two heaps are played together, a position is a P-position iff the XOR of their Grundy values is 0 (Sprague–Grundy theorem). Our single line is the simplest case.

Zermelo's theorem

Statement

In a finite two-player game of perfect information with no chance and no draws, exactly one of the players has a winning strategy.

Proof idea: backward induction on the game tree. Each leaf is a win for someone; each inner node inherits “win for the mover” if some child is a win for the mover. Since there are no draws, every node ends up labelled for exactly one player.

Because Amanda picks who goes first, she gets to be the player with the winning strategy at the root: that's why the answer is “all n” even without the arithmetic.

Subgame-perfect equilibrium

Economists call backward induction's output a subgame-perfect Nash equilibrium (course MGMT4100 territory).

What it means here

Amanda's strategy is optimal not just from the start but from every position where she may find herself, even ones reached by Bodil's mistakes. That's exactly the requirement “guaranteed strategy”.

Why it's credible

There are no empty threats. Every move Amanda promises (“I'll take 7 − k”) is also the best move when the time comes. That's what makes it a robust guarantee rather than a hope.
