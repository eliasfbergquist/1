If nobody chooses who starts

Amanda's real power in the task is picking the starter. Without it, a random first player wins when n is not a multiple of m + 1: probability about m/(m + 1).

Reading the curve

Bigger m → more luck for the starter

With m = 1 the game is just parity: half the time you start in a trap. With m = 6, the starter wins 6 times in 7 (86%). Great for the first player — but a good reason the assignment lets Amanda decide rather than toss a coin.

Why it holds for every m

  1. Pairing. If Bodil takes k ≤ m, Amanda takes (m + 1) − k, also between 1 and m. Every round removes exactly m + 1.
  2. Opening. Amanda first takes n mod (m + 1), or lets Bodil start if that is 0.
  3. Ending. Bodil always faces a positive multiple of m + 1 — never ≤ m, so she can't finish. Amanda can.
