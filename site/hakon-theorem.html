A completely serious proof that the fictional Håkon is a P-position, followed by an experiment that is, unfortunately for him, real: ten thousand simulated games using Håkon's play-style.

Theorem (Håkon)

Whoever has to move against Håkon is not in a trap. Håkon, however, always is.

Proof

  1. Define the Håkon number H as the number of prizes Håkon thinks he can take safely. Observe H = 3.Empirically established by watching him.

  2. Lemma 1. 3 ≢ 0 (mod 7), hence Håkon is not himself a multiple of seven.This is the only inconvenient step. We shall ignore it.

  3. Lemma 2. Let G be any game involving Håkon. By Zermelo, exactly one player has a winning strategy. It is not Håkon.Proof by experience.

  4. Lemma 3. Every move Håkon can make leads to a position where Amanda wins.Check by listing: take 1 → loses. Take 2 → loses. Take 3 → loses. And so on. Exhaustive, if depressing.

  5. Conclusion. A position in which every move loses is by definition a P-position. Håkon has no winning move. Therefore Håkon is a P-position, a trap.Or, equivalently, Håkon ≡ 0 (mod 7) in spirit.

Q.E.D. ∎ (and then some)

The experiment: Håkon vs a perfect Amanda

For every n from 1 to 40 we simulate games between Håkon's play-style (the same bot as on Beat Håkon) and a perfect Amanda. Gold: Håkon's win rate if he is forced to start. Red: his win rate when Amanda chooses who starts.

Håkon forced to startAmanda picks the starter
Reading the chart

The red bars are flat zero: no matter what, Amanda picks the correct starter and wins. The gold bars show that Håkon wins only when he starts at a non-multiple of 7 and happens to stumble onto the right take (for n ≤ 6 he simply grabs everything). At 7, 14, 21, 28, 35 the gold bar is also zero: there he is in a trap from move one. Bars shrink as n grows because he has to stumble correctly more than once.
