The cast

👧

Amanda

Class 5A. Cares about exactly one prize in the world. Gets to choose who starts.

🧒

Bodil

Class 7B. Would also like the book, but would be fine without it. Plays to take it if she can.

📖

The book

Fishes of Edo. Laid out first, as prize 1: the very last prize to be picked.

The rules

n

A line of prizes

An unknown number n ≥ 1 of prizes is laid out in a row. Prize 1 is the book, at the front.

6

Take 1 to 6

Players alternate. On your turn you take at least 1 and at most 6 prizes — from the end of the row.

⇄

Amanda picks the opener

Once she sees n, Amanda decides: does she begin, or does Bodil?

★

Guarantee

Amanda needs a method that guarantees the book, whatever Bodil does. The rest doesn't matter.

Try the rules by hand

This is hot-seat: you control both players. Click any of the last six prizes (or a number button) to take them. Switch who starts, change n, and get a feel for it.

Why the book is special

Prizes come off the end of the row, and the book is at the front. So the book can only be picked up in the very last take — the one that empties the line.

That turns the whole question into a much simpler one:

Whoever makes the last take gets the book.

Nothing else about the prizes matters. We never care who gets the candy, the toys or the ornaments, only who removes the final group.

Reformulation

A "last mover wins" game

A pile of n counters. Remove 1–6 per turn. The player who takes the last counter wins. Mathematicians call this a subtraction game, and it is one of the best-understood games there is.

What's actually being asked

1

For which n is there a guaranteed plan?

Not just one n: for every possible number of prizes, can Amanda force the book? The more values you can handle, the better.

2

Who should start?

The plan has two parts: the decision about who opens, and a rule for what to take on each of Amanda's turns.

3

Why does the plan work?

A guarantee needs a reason. The strategy page shows the plan; the proof page shows why nothing Bodil does can break it.

Spoiler

The answer is: every n. If you want to figure out why for yourself first, play with the sandbox above and the game and come back when you're ready.
